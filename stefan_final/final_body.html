The briefwhat was asked

Make the titles and opening sequence of a new film, up to about two minutes long, with all footage shot by us. We chose to work as a pair instead of alone, to combine our skills and ideas.

Chapter 04 / Genre

Why actiongenre selection

Our early ideas were horror, thriller and action. We both wanted to work in action and agreed we could make a gripping opening in it, and my solid editing skills suit the genre best. Horror and thriller ideas stayed in as tension and mystery, not as the main genre.

Genre
Action
Sub-genre
Spy / crime action: secret agents, a stolen object, an organisation behind it all.
Hybrid
Action thriller, with the dark comedy of the two agents' banter.

Conventions

Mind map
Settingscity · industrial

Big cities, rooftops, alleys, car parks, warehouses, motorways, wet streets and neon.

Charactershero · villain · partner

A skilled, calm hero with a past; a powerful villain; a partner or sidekick; henchmen; an organisation giving orders.

Plotsmission · revenge · chase

A mission or heist, revenge, a race against time, a stolen object everyone wants (a MacGuffin), double-crosses.

Action conventionswhat the audience expects
Iconographyguns · suits · cars

Guns, sharp suits and sunglasses, fast cars, phones and messages, briefcases, watches and countdowns, explosions.

Camera & editingfast · close · moving

Fast cutting, handheld or tracking shots, slow motion at key moments, low angles for power, split screens.

Soundloud · driving · punchy

A driving score or a well-known song, heavy sound effects (gunshots, engines, hits), short one-liners.

A short history

1900s to now
  1. 1940s–60sAction grows through westerns, war, martial-arts and spy films. Dr. No (1962) sets the template for the modern spy film.
  2. 1970s–80sThe blockbuster era: famous heroes, bigger stunts and fights. Rocky, Raiders of the Lost Ark, Die Hard.
  3. 1990s–2000sCGI and complex editing bring new visual styles, led by The Matrix (1999).
  4. TodayReal stunts mixed with CGI and ambitious camera work, and more complex heroes. John Wick, Mad Max: Fury Road, Extraction.

Genre icons

Directors & actors

Christopher Nolan

Non-linear stories, practical effects and huge-scale images. Memento, The Dark Knight trilogy, Inception, Interstellar, Oppenheimer (Best Director and Best Picture, 2024).

Quentin Tarantino

Self-taught in a video store; famous for sharp dialogue, non-linear plots and music. Reservoir Dogs, Pulp Fiction (Palme d'Or), Kill Bill, Django Unchained.

Bruce Lee

Made martial-arts films popular worldwide and changed fight choreography. Enter the Dragon (1973).

Arnold Schwarzenegger

Defined the powerful 1980s action hero. The Terminator (1984), Predator (1987).

What we take from themNolan: a non-linear start (our villain and nightmare come before the routine). Tarantino: two suited partners with dark banter, and music as a main character. Bruce Lee and Schwarzenegger show the classic strong hero, which we deliberately avoid: our agents never show off their strength.

Chapter 07 / Ideas

Ideasfirst ideas

Genrehorror · thriller · action

We started with three genres: horror, thriller and action. We both wanted to work in action and felt we could make the most gripping opening in it. Horror and thriller ideas stayed in as tension and mystery.

The diskthe mystery object

A stolen disk is what the whole film is about. Nobody explains what is on it, which creates an enigma. The villain is seen with it in the first shots and again in the credits.

The agentsseven code names, one per day

A serious organisation has seven special agents, each named after a day of the week. Monday and Friday are put on a case together for the first time ever, which tells the audience the case is serious. Always in suits, always armed, friendly but menacing, with dark jokes between them.

Our film openingfirst ideas
Story pitchesThe Drop · The Courier · Twelve Minutes

Our first story ideas: a handover that goes wrong, a courier carrying something they should not open, and a heist against the clock. Each one gave a reason for a chase or a standoff.

One-song opening"Six Days" as the score

The whole opening plays over one song, DJ Shadow's "Six Days" (remix with Mos Def), slowing down and stopping under the film title at 2:15. Cuts land on the beat, like Baby Driver, and in one shot the song is playing in Monday's own headphones.

Our picktwo agents, one stolen disk

The opening cuts between the two agents' very different mornings while chaos happens around them, and they never even turn their heads. It ends just as they are about to meet.

Chapter 08 / Theory

Applying theoryto Dirty Deeds

Barthes

Enigma & action codes

Enigmas: Who is the man in the car on the hill? What is on the disk? Why are Monday and Friday paired for the first time? What does “You won't be alone this time” mean? Action codes: the chase past the bus, the fight behind her car, the gunshot in the alley and the car pulling up all tell us something is about to happen.

Todorov

Equilibrium

The opening shows the equilibrium: two calm, separate morning routines. But the disruption has already happened (the disk is stolen, shown in the villain shots and the nightmare). The opening ends at the recognition: the message “Partner: Friday”. The rest of the film would be the repair and a new equilibrium, the two of them as a team.

Propp

Character types

Villain: the man in the car with the disk. Heroes: Monday and Friday. Dispatcher: the organisation that sends the 9:00 message. The “princess” / prize: the disk. Henchman: the robber in the alley. Unusually, we have two heroes, and the audience cannot yet tell if one might be a false hero.

Lévi-Strauss

Binary oppositions

Monday vs Friday · his gritty flat and bus vs her elegant flat and car · poor vs rich · cold grey vs warm gold · night vs morning · calm vs chaos · order vs disorder.

Representation

Reinforce or challenge

We challenge the female sidekick: Friday is an equal, the richer and more composed of the two, and she fixes her lipstick while a fight happens behind her: a countertype who is never rescued. We also challenge the muscle-bound hero: Monday wins by ignoring the danger, not by showing strength. We reinforce the stylish suited agent, so the audience still recognises the genre.

Chapter 09 / Planning

Planningshot by shot

The treatment, the opening planned to the music, then the script, storyboard and sound plan.

Treatment

The film in one page

TitleDirty Deeds

LoglineTwo agents who have never worked together must recover a stolen disk, and the city is falling apart around them.

GenreSpy / crime action, hybrid with thriller and dark comedy.

Audience16–30, fans of stylish, music-driven action.

The openingAt night, a man in a parked car on a hill holds a stolen disk; we never see his face. After the titles, a nightmare wakes Monday. We follow his grey, ordinary morning: kettle, suit, bus, headphones. A message says he won't be alone this time. A match cut takes us to Friday's elegant flat and her car. Around them a chase, a fight and a robbery happen; neither turns their head. Both get the same message: their partner's name. Monday sits on a bench at 8:59 as a car pulls up. The title appears.

CharactersMonday: calm, working-class, worn suit, lives alone, always listening to music. Friday: elegant, rich, precise, never late. The villain: only ever a silhouette and a gloved hand.

StyleNo dialogue. Cuts on the beat of DJ Shadow's “Six Days (Remix)”. His world grey and cold, hers warm. Split screens and glitch cuts join their two stories.

Risk assessment

Keeping everyone safe
RiskWhoLevelHow we reduce it
Replica guns in publicCast, crew, publicHighOnly use them in private places or with permission; keep them hidden in a bag between takes; tell the location owner and school beforehand; never point them at the public.
Filming near roads and carsCast, crewHighFilm from the pavement, never stand in the road, use a quiet street for the car pulling up, someone watches for traffic.
The fight sceneActorsMediumRehearse slowly first, no real contact, clear the area of hard objects, sell the hits with the camera angle and sound.
Filming on a busCrew, passengersMediumUse smaller camera only, don't block the aisle, don't film passengers' faces without asking.

ProblemPlan B
